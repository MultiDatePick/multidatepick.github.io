Create Salesforce Tasks From a Calendar: Pick the Days, Save Once

Click every day that needs a task, type the subject, and save. One standard Salesforce Task per day, linked to the record, with no Flow, no Apex and no code.

Published November 8, 2026 · MultiDatePick

"How do I create the same task on several dates?" "Can I create multiple tasks at once in Salesforce?" "How do I see my tasks on a calendar?" The New Task button saves one task with one due date. A recurring series repeats on a fixed schedule. A follow-up plan of four calls on four chosen days is still four trips through the same form.

MultiDatePick puts a calendar on the record page. Click the days, type the subject and click Save: one Salesforce Task per day, each linked to the record, with its status, priority and comments filled in. They are ordinary Tasks, so they show in the Activity timeline, in reports and in every list view. No Flow, no Apex.

What you get

One task per day

Every day you click becomes its own Task, with that day as its due date. Ten days, ten tasks, one save.

Linked to the record

Each task is related to the page's record: an account, opportunity, case or custom record, or the contact or lead it is for.

Filled in for you

The subject you type, the status you pick, and any other field such as priority or comments, on every new task.

Any days at all

Click scattered days, drag across a run of them, or add a recurring pattern such as every other Tuesday.

Tasks on a calendar

Existing tasks show in their status colors. Hover a day for the subject and status, or write each subject in its day.

Change them later

Edit Mode moves a task to another day, changes its status or deletes it, for one task or many at once.

Pick the days, save once

The calendar sits on the record page and shows the tasks the record already has, in the color of their status. Planning a series of customer check-ins takes four steps:

  1. Click the days. Pick each day that needs a task. A picked day turns orange; click it again to drop it.
  2. Type the subject. The Record Label box holds the subject every new task gets, such as Customer check-in.
  3. Choose the status. Not Started, In Progress or any value of your Task Status picklist.
  4. Click Save. One Task per day is created and linked to the record. The days take the status color straight away.

Point at any day to see its task's subject and status, and open the task from there. Days that already have a task can take another one too, when Allow Multiple Per Day is on.

Ask for every detail in a pop-up

A subject and a status are often not enough. Turn on the Record details pop-up in the Setup Wizard and Create Tasks first opens a form with the fields you choose: priority, comments, any field on Task. It fills every picked day the same way, or, with Each day, gives one day its own subject while the rest share the others.

The fields, which ones are required, their default values and their width all come from the wizard's Fields screen, and the pop-up can also open the moment a day is clicked, one task at a time. Only fields the person can create are offered, and the fields Salesforce requires are marked required.

Real Salesforce Tasks, in the Activity timeline

Nothing here is a copy or a custom object. Each day saves a standard Task, so it behaves like any task your team creates by hand: it shows in the record's Activity timeline, on the Tasks tab, in reports and list views, and in the assignee's to-do list. On a contact or lead the task links through Name; on an account, opportunity, case or custom object, through Related To.

Every task on one calendar

The same component works as a task calendar. On an app or Home page, turn on Show All Records and it shows every Task you can see; add a Record Filter such as OwnerId=ME and each person sees their own. With Show Record Names in Each Day, each subject is written in its day, in the color of its status, and the pills above the calendar filter by status.

Already have tasks spread across an account's contacts and opportunities? A Rollup Field brings them onto the account's calendar too. See how the account calendar shows related records.

When the days follow a pattern

Some task plans repeat: a call every other Tuesday, a report on the last Friday of the month, a review on the 2nd and 4th Friday. Add Recurring Pattern fills those days in for you, shows the dates before it adds them, and stops at the end date you set. Each day still saves as its own Task, so you can move or skip one without touching the rest.

More patterns, and why a date range cannot hold them, in recurring date patterns in Salesforce.

Set it up in the Setup Wizard

No code, and no Flow to build. The Setup Wizard writes the configuration and the component reads it:

  1. Choose where the calendar goes. Open the MultiDatePick Setup Wizard tab, pick the record page (Account here) and the one record per date shape.
  2. Save each pick as a Task. On the Fields screen click Task, then check the link to the page: Related To ID (WhatId) on an Account, or Name ID (WhoId) on a Contact or Lead. The wizard fills in Due Date Only (ActivityDate) and Subject.
  3. Fill in the rest. Type a default subject, untick Append Date/Time to Record Name to keep subjects clean, and enter Default Field Values such as Priority=High. Save the config under a name like Account_Tasks.
  4. Drop the component on the page. In Lightning App Builder, add the MultiDatePick Dates component to the Account page and enter the name in Config Name.

Status colors come from your Task Status values, so Completed can be green and Deferred grey. Change the config later and every page that uses it picks the change up on its next load.

Who creates tasks this way

Anyone whose plan is a handful of chosen days rather than a single due date:

Account managersQuarterly check-ins and site visits for the year, planned on the account in one go.
Sales follow-upCalls on day 2, day 7 and day 14 after a demo, created on the opportunity before the meeting ends.
Customer successOnboarding sessions on the days agreed with the customer, each one a task with its own status.
Compliance and financeFiling and review deadlines that fall on fixed dates, assigned to the person who owns them.
NonprofitsThank-you calls and stewardship touches for a major donor, spread across the season.
Service teamsFollow-up checks after a case closes, on the days the customer asked for.

Questions people ask

How do I create multiple tasks at once in Salesforce?

Put the MultiDatePick Dates component on the record page with Task as the object it saves. Click every day that needs a task, type the subject, and click Save. MultiDatePick creates one standard Salesforce Task per day, each with its own due date and linked to the record, in a single save. No Flow and no Apex are involved.

Can I create Salesforce tasks on dates that do not follow a pattern?

Yes. You click the exact days you need, such as the 3rd, the 11th and the 24th, and each one becomes a task. When the days do follow a pattern, the Add Recurring Pattern button fills them in for you: every Tuesday, every other week, or the 1st or last weekday of each month.

Which record pages can create tasks this way?

Any record that tasks can be related to. Accounts, Opportunities, Cases and custom objects with activities link through Related To (WhatId). Contacts and Leads link through Name (WhoId). On a Contact page, Salesforce also fills in the contact's account, so the new tasks show on the account too.

Can every new task get the same priority, status or assignee?

Yes. The status picker sets the Status of the tasks being saved. Default Field Values fills any other field on every new task, for example Priority=High, a Comments line, or OwnerId set to a user's Id to assign the tasks to that person. Without it, tasks are assigned to the person who saves them, as usual in Salesforce.

Can I see my Salesforce tasks on a calendar?

Yes. On a record page the calendar shows that record's tasks in their status colors. On an app or Home page, turn on Show All Records and set Record Filter to OwnerId=ME for a calendar of your own tasks, with each subject written in its day. Hover a day for the details, or add the Agenda view for a list of them.
