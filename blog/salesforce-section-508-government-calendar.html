An Accessible Salesforce Calendar for Government Teams

Agencies on Salesforce book inspections, appointments, facilities and field staff every day. The federal deadline for making those pages accessible just moved to April 2027. Here is what it asks of the calendar, and how MultiDatePick answers it.

Published October 11, 2026 · MultiDatePick

In April 2024 the Department of Justice set a firm technical standard for state and local government websites and apps under ADA Title II: WCAG 2.1 Level AA. In April 2026 it gave agencies one more year to get there. That year is the window to fix the parts of your Salesforce org that residents and staff actually click, and the scheduling calendar is near the top of that list.

The dates that apply now

An interim final rule published April 20, 2026 extended both compliance dates by one year. The standard itself did not change.

Public entity Comply by Standard
Serving 50,000 people or more April 26, 2027 WCAG 2.1 AA
Under 50,000, and special district governments April 26, 2028 WCAG 2.1 AA

Two things are easy to miss. First, many articles still quote April 2026, which is out of date. Second, the Justice Department is explicit that the obligation to make services accessible did not pause: the dates set when the specific technical standard becomes enforceable, not when accessibility starts to matter. Federal agencies were never on this clock at all; they answer to Section 508, which already requires WCAG 2.0 AA, and every one of those criteria is also part of 2.1 AA.

Where scheduling shows up in a government org

Almost every public service on Salesforce has a calendar in it.

Inspections

Homeowners pick a day for a building or permit inspection.

Appointments

Residents book licensing, benefits and records visits.

Facility rentals

Families reserve pavilions, ballfields and community rooms.

Clinics

Vaccination slots with a fixed number of seats per hour.

Field staff

Inspectors, caseworkers and poll workers scheduled across a month.

Closures

Meeting rooms blocked for holidays and maintenance.

Salesforce being accessible does not cover your calendar. A third-party component draws its own markup, so its accessibility is the vendor's job. Resident pages on Experience Cloud fall under Title II, staff pages fall under Section 508, and both need a calendar a keyboard or screen reader user can finish. New to VPATs? The VPAT marks two criteria as Partially Supports, and says exactly why. Contrast: the default palette meets 4.5:1, but admins can choose their own status colors, and a vendor cannot guarantee a color it did not pick. Reflow: at 320 pixels wide the month grid keeps its seven columns and narrows rather than stacking. Procurement reviewers trust a report that explains its partials over a wall of unexplained Supports.

The standard date field is accessible, but it holds one date, so it cannot run a booking. An external tool can book, but residents' names, addresses and appointment history then live in someone else's system, which is a second security review and a second accessibility review. Getting ready before April 2027

  1. Inventory every date picker your public touches. Experience Cloud pages first, then staff Screen Flows. Note which ones are standard fields and which are third-party or custom components.
  2. Request a current VPAT for every third-party component. Check the report date and that it was tested on the packaged version you actually install.
  3. Test the booking path with a keyboard alone. Pick a date, choose a time, save. If you cannot finish without a mouse, neither can a resident who relies on a keyboard or a screen reader.
