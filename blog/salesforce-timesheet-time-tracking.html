Time Tracking in Salesforce: Timesheets on a Calendar

Log hours as blocks on a week, tie each one to a project, total them with a roll-up and approve the team's time in bulk. Every entry is a Salesforce record, set up with clicks.

Published November 17, 2026 · MultiDatePick

"How do I track time in Salesforce?" "Is there a Salesforce timesheet?" "How do I log the same hours on several days?" A time entry in Salesforce is a record with one date field. Logging a week means opening the New form for every block of every day, and nothing on the screen shows the week as a week.

MultiDatePick puts a time entry calendar on the weekly Time Sheet record. Click the days, drag the hours once and save: one Time Entry record per block, linked to the time sheet and its project. Statuses run the approval, a Billing picklist splits billable from non-billable time, and two standard Salesforce fields add up the hours. No Apex.

What you get

Same hours, many days

Pick the days and drag the hours once. Every picked day gets them, or each day gets its own.

One record per block

9 to 12 and 1 to 5 save as two Time Entries, each with its own start, end and project.

Billable or not

A Billing picklist sits beside the task. Billable and non-billable hours add up separately.

Approval statuses

Draft, Submitted, Approved and Rejected, each in its own color, with a filter pill for each.

Week, Calendar, Agenda

The week by the hour, the month at a glance, or a list of entries with hours and billing.

Approve in bulk

Edit Mode approves a whole day of the team's entries at once, and a Submit week button runs a Flow.

Log a week in one save

The calendar sits on the person's weekly Time Sheet and opens on the Week view, with the time already logged in its status colors. Filling the rest of the week takes five steps:

  1. Pick the days. Click Wednesday, Thursday and Friday, or use Add Recurring Pattern for every weekday up to a date.
  2. Drag the hours. Drag 9:00 to 12:00, then 1:00 to 5:00 PM, on the time grid. The same hours go on every picked day.
  3. Name the task. Type what the time was for, such as Client workshop. Billing starts on Billable; switch it for internal work.
  4. Choose the project. Save Time opens a short pop-up that asks for the project, so every entry is tied to one.
  5. Save. Each block becomes a Time Entry in Draft, linked to the time sheet. The Week view shows them straight away.

Hover any block to see its project, hours and billing, and open the record from there. When each day has different hours, turn on Allow Different Times Per Date: every picked day then gets its own time grid, grouped by week.

Add up the hours with two Salesforce fields

Totals come from the fields every Salesforce admin already uses, so they show on the record, in list views and in reports. Two steps in Object Manager:

  1. An Hours formula on the Time Entry. A Number formula, (End_Time__c - Start_Time__c) / 3600000, turns each block into hours: 9:00 to 12:00 is 3.
  2. Roll-ups on the weekly Time Sheet. With Time Entry in a master-detail relationship to Time Sheet, a Roll-Up Summary field sums Hours into Total Hours. Filtered copies give Approved Hours (Status equals Approved), Billable Hours and Non-billable Hours.

Billable and non-billable time

Add a Billing picklist to the Time Entry with two values, Billable and Non-billable, and make Billable its default. Set it as the calendar's Second Picklist Field: it then appears next to the task box, so the person picks it while logging time, and every save carries it. Then:

  • See it on every entry. Add the field to Hover Popover Fields and Agenda Fields, and the hover card and the Agenda list show Billable or Non-billable beside the hours.
  • Change many at once. A Mark non-billable button in Edit Mode runs a Flow on every picked Draft entry, for the morning that turned out to be internal training.
  • Total each kind. The Billable Hours and Non-billable Hours roll-ups filter on Billing. Time Entries are ordinary records, so a standard report can group them by Billing and Project too.

Approval keeps the status colors, and billing keeps its own field, so both show together on every entry.

Submit, approve, reject

The Status Field carries the approval: Draft, Submitted, Approved and Rejected, each with its own color and a pill above the calendar that hides or shows it. Selectable Statuses set to Draft means a person's new entries always start as Draft.

When the week is done, the person turns on Edit Mode, picks the days and presses Submit week. It is a custom action that runs a Flow, and it counts only the Draft entries among the ones picked.

The manager works from a Team Time page: the same calendar on an app page with Show All Records on, so every person's entries are there, with the employee's name on each one. Edit Mode lists a day's entries for the whole team; Change status to Approved and Apply Changes approves them in one step. Rejected works the same way, and the person sees the gray Rejected block on their own week.

A split shift, an on-call evening, an extra day

Plans change after the week is logged. Pick a day that already has time on it and its blocks show as taken in the time grid; drag a new block beside them, such as an evening on-call from 6:00 to 7:30 PM, and it saves as one more entry. Extra days work the same way: click them and save. Max Bookings per Day sets how many separate blocks a person can pick on one day, with 0 for no limit.

Questions people ask

Does Salesforce have a timesheet?

Not for everyday time tracking in Sales Cloud or Service Cloud. Most teams build a Time Entry custom object with a date, a start and an end time, and a link to a weekly Time Sheet. MultiDatePick puts a calendar on that Time Sheet, so people log their week by clicking days and dragging hours, and every block saves as a Time Entry record.

How do I log the same hours on several days in Salesforce?

With the MultiDatePick Dates & Times component, click the days, then drag the hours once, for example 9:00 to 12:00 and 1:00 to 5:00 PM. Every picked day gets the same hours and each block saves as its own record. For a whole week, Add Recurring Pattern picks every weekday up to an end date. Turn on Allow Different Times Per Date when each day needs its own hours.

How do I total hours on a Salesforce timesheet?

Add an Hours formula field to the Time Entry, (End_Time__c - Start_Time__c) / 3600000, and roll it up to the weekly Time Sheet with a Roll-Up Summary field over a master-detail relationship. Add filtered roll-ups for Approved Hours, Billable Hours and Non-billable Hours. All of them are point-and-click Salesforce fields and update as entries are saved.

How do I track billable and non-billable hours in Salesforce?

Add a Billing picklist with Billable and Non-billable to the Time Entry and set it as the calendar's Second Picklist Field. People pick it next to the task while logging time, Billable comes in as the default, and it shows on the hover card and in the Agenda. Two filtered roll-ups on the Time Sheet total each kind, and a Mark non-billable button in Edit Mode changes many entries at once.

Can a manager approve timesheets in bulk in Salesforce?

Yes. On a Team Time page that shows every entry, the manager turns on Edit Mode, picks the days, and sets Change status to Approved for every listed entry at once. Custom buttons that run a Flow, such as Submit week for the person logging time, appear in Edit Mode too and act only on the statuses they are set for.

Can someone log two separate shifts on one day?

Yes. Pick a day that already has time logged and its blocks show as taken in the time grid; drag a new block, such as an evening on-call, and it saves as one more entry. Max Bookings per Day sets how many separate blocks a person can pick on one day, with 0 for no limit.
