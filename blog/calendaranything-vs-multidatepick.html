CalendarAnything vs MultiDatePick: Which Salesforce Calendar Fits Your Team?

CalendarAnything puts Salesforce records on calendars to look at. MultiDatePick shows them too, and lets people pick dates, book time and save records with the rules enforced. For scheduling work in Salesforce, MultiDatePick is the better choice.

Published November 2, 2026 · MultiDatePick

If you search for a Salesforce calendar app, CalendarAnything comes up early. It has been on the AppExchange for years, and it is good at what it was built for: putting many kinds of Salesforce records on shared calendars that teams browse, filter and print. MultiDatePick starts from the other end: the moment someone picks dates or books a time, and the records that come out of it.

Both are native, both work with standard and custom objects, and both are configured without code. But a calendar you can only look at leaves the real work to someone else: booking without double booking, picking many dates at once, letting customers schedule themselves. That is where MultiDatePick pulls ahead.

Why MultiDatePick is the better choice

Many dates, one action

Click scattered days, drag ranges or add a recurring pattern, and save them as child records in one go.

Booking that holds

Live capacity badges, conflict checks on save and several resources in one booking.

Rules on every pick

Notice, booking window, buffers, business hours and closures, enforced on pick and on drag.

Inside Screen Flows

Picks become Flow variables, so a flow can route, approve or save them its own way.

Public booking

Guests book on an Experience Cloud site with the same rules as your staff.

Six record shapes

One record per date, per range, per time block, per resource, or a parent with sub-blocks.

When CalendarAnything might still fit

If all you need is to browse many calendars in Gantt or Kanban layouts, print them, or sync them two ways with Google or Outlook, CalendarAnything covers that. If your team schedules anything (rooms, people, visits, time off), those are extras, not reasons: the scheduling itself is what MultiDatePick does and CalendarAnything does not.

Switching is low risk

MultiDatePick saves ordinary Salesforce records on your own objects. Reports, flows, list views and anything else that reads those objects keep working, so trying it costs a config, not a migration.

Questions people ask

Is MultiDatePick a CalendarAnything alternative?

Yes, and for scheduling it is the stronger one: multi-date picking, capacity and conflict checks, public booking pages and Screen Flow outputs are built in, alongside six calendar views. CalendarAnything adds Gantt and Kanban layouts and calendar sync, which matter only if browsing is the whole job.

Does MultiDatePick sync with Outlook or Google Calendar?

It offers an Add to Calendar file after each save, which opens in Outlook, Google Calendar and Apple Calendar. It does not keep a two-way sync.

Can MultiDatePick show records from any object?

Yes. Any standard or custom object with a Date or Date/Time field, including Task and Event, and records reached through up to five lookups with the Rollup Field.
